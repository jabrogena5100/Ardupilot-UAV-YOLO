What am I looking at?

The task. One UAV flies a 1 mile × 1 mile area (a 10 × 10 grid) and has to find a wildfire it was not told about. The fire is simulated; the UAV sees the world only through a camera whose images are fed to a neural network.

Three arms, same scenario. Baseline flies a fixed coverage patrol with no detector. Model A is a smoke detector trained on synthetic images; Model B is trained on real (Boreal) images. Both use the same reactive rule: when the detector reports smoke, fly to that cell and investigate before resuming the patrol.

How to read it. Orange/grey cells are the true fire, which the UAV cannot see directly (hide it with “UAV’s view”). Blue cells are what the UAV has flown over. Green and red circles are detector observations, coloured by comparing them with the true fire after the fact (the UAV itself cannot tell them apart; “UAV’s view” turns them neutral); cyan and magenta boxes are the cells it was sent to investigate. Press Play, or drag the slider; all panels share one clock.

Limits. Single UAV only. The policy is reactive and detection-triggered; there is no probability map. 8 scenarios on 4 ignition cells: results are descriptive, not statistical claims. Model A is tested on the same synthetic imagery style it was trained on; Model B is not. Smoke is a flat billboard, not a volume.
