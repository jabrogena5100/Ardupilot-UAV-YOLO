# Level 3 final paired evaluation (2026-11-09), corrected after the read-only audit

**Status: descriptive results prepared for independent Codex review. Not declared scientifically validated.** Revision note: the first version of this document (commit `7e203a1`) contained a provenance error (it said every run had `git_dirty: true`), reported unadjusted scenario-level p-values as if the 8 scenarios were independent, and treated censored times as means. Those are corrected here; the audit that found them is summarised in `docs/handoffs/level3_final_audit.md`.

**Purpose.** Does the detector's training domain (Model A synthetic, Model B real/Boreal v2) change what a UAV does in ArduPilot SITL, against a no-detector patrol baseline? Protocol (frozen before any held-out run): `level3_methodology.md` section 15.3. Everything below is computed from recorded artifacts; generated tables come from `eval/level3_analyze.py` and `eval/level3_report_tables.py` (inputs and outputs listed under Artifacts).

## 1. Design and what the "policy" is
8 held-out scenarios (2001, 2002, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2012, 2014, 2015) x 7 arms (baseline; A0, A1, A2; B2s0, B2s1, B2s2) = 56 runs, T = 600 s, single UAV, oracle off, 10 x 10 grid. Per-arm thresholds frozen (A 0.30-0.45, B 0.01-0.02).
**Only 4 distinct ignition cells exist** (`HELD_IGNITION_V2`, methodology 15.2): (3,2) in scenarios 2001 and 2005, (2,1) in 2002 and 2012, (1,0) in 2004 and 2014, (0,1) in 2006 and 2015. Within a cell the scenarios differ in fire seed and texture slot but share the ignition location, so for time-to-reach metrics they are partly pseudo-replicates. **The ignition cell is therefore the primary cluster (n = 4); scenarios (n = 8) are descriptive.**

**What flies the UAV is a reactive, detection-triggered investigation policy, not a probability-map planner.** Implemented and used (`planning/priority.py`, `swarm_agent.py` step 5): each detection above `conf_threshold` is stored as per-cell evidence with a 20 s lifetime; a cell's score is `max(conf)` plus a small repeat bonus; when any live evidence exists the UAV flies to the highest-scoring cell (ties by distance) **before** continuing the coverage patrol; on arrival the cell is cleared and re-flagging of that cell is suppressed for 60 s. Detections are scored against fog's ground truth at ingest (one-cell truth radius). Evidence of the mechanism: in the inspected runs (`l3-A0-2001`, `l3-B2s0-2004`) every INVESTIGATE target is a detected cell, the baseline has no detections and no investigations, and `oracle_hazards` is false in all 58 run manifests.
**Not implemented** (originally proposed in `CLAUDE.md`, Level 3 data flow): a geographic probability map (`mapping/probability_map.py` is an empty stub), detection probabilities combined over time or across looks (no log-odds update, no negative evidence, no use of Level 2 sensitivity/specificity), a priority-region decision from such a map, and any confidence-aware trade-off between investigating and covering (a detection at the 0.01 threshold floor diverts the UAV exactly like a confident one, the investigation always preempts coverage). Consequently the results measure how a fixed reactive policy turns each detector's output (including its false alarms) into behaviour; they do not measure the benefit of a probabilistic monitoring planner.

## 3. Statistical approach and what it can support
The 4 ignition cells are the primary clusters. For each contrast the per-scenario differences (family value = mean over seeds) are averaged within a cell, giving 4 cell differences; the descriptive interval is a bootstrap over the 4 cells, and `p` is the exact sign-flip test over the 4 cell differences. **With 4 clusters the smallest possible two-sided p is 0.125, so no contrast can be significant at 0.05**, with or without Holm correction (16 contrasts; all Holm p >= 0.125). The p-values are reported only to make this explicit. Scenario-level numbers (8 differences, sign-flip floor 0.0078) are descriptive because scenarios within a cell are not independent. The earlier "p = 0.0078" statements are withdrawn. The supported evidence is direction and consistency: how many cells and scenarios have the same sign, and the size of the difference. Seeds are not pooled: a family value in a scenario is the mean of its 3 seeds, and seed spread is reported separately (section 5). The set of reported contrasts is the full set in `cluster_stats.md`; none was chosen after the fact, but with this sample size every interval is crude and uncertain.

## 4. Results (descriptive)
Family mean +- SD over the 3 seeds, each seed value the mean over 8 scenarios (`seed_summary.md`; time metrics are lower bounds because of censoring, section 6):

| metric | baseline | Model A | Model B |
|---|---|---|---|
| first true detection (s, lower bound) | no detector | 177.1 +- 7.0 | 296.6 +- 48.0 |
| first visit of ignition cell (s, lower bound; **15/56 censored**) | 221.4 | 268.0 +- 31.1 | 487.7 +- 14.4 |
| grid coverage at 600 s | 0.209 | 0.183 +- 0.003 | 0.147 +- 0.007 |
| false detection **observations** / run | 0 | 0.3 +- 0.2 | 55.7 +- 23.0 |
| distinct cells with a false detection / run | 0 | 0.2 +- 0.2 | 6.4 +- 1.2 |
| INVESTIGATE log lines on burned-adjacent targets (about seconds) | 0 | 173.7 +- 10.1 | 237.1 +- 41.2 |
| INVESTIGATE log lines on false targets (about seconds) | 0 | 1.6 +- 2.7 | 80.6 +- 47.8 |

Direction (cell-level counts, then scenario-level), A relative to B: first true detection earlier in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (mean cell difference -119.5 s; descriptive cell-bootstrap interval -170.9 to -68.0); ignition-cell first visit earlier 4/4 and 8/8 (-219.7 s, a lower bound on the gap because B has 14 censored runs); coverage higher 4/4 and 8/8 (+0.035); false detection observations lower 4/4 and 8/8 (-55.4); distinct false cells lower 4/4 and 8/8 (-6.2). Time on true targets did not differ consistently (cells 2/2, scenarios 3/5; -63.4 lines, interval -176.3 to +26.4). Against the baseline: B reached the ignition cell later in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (+266.3 s, lower bound); A reached it later in 4/4 cells but only 6/8 scenarios (+46.6 s; in scenarios 2012 and 2015 A was earlier, by 32 s and 54 s); both perception arms covered less than the patrol in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (A -0.026, B -0.061). Scenario-level differences and the 2005-excluded sensitivity are in `cluster_stats.md`.

**What these results support:** Model A and Model B produce different behaviour under the same reactive policy, consistently in direction across all 4 cells: Model B (cross-domain, thresholds at the detector floor) detects later, raises many more false detection observations and false-target investigations, reaches the ignition cell later or not at all within 600 s, and covers less. On average neither perception arm found the ignition cell sooner than the plain patrol (A was earlier in 2 of 8 scenarios), so **no monitoring benefit of perception is shown here**; this is a property of the policy (investigations preempt coverage) as much as of the detectors.
**What they do not support:** significance claims; a quantity of the Model B delay (censoring); a statement about real flights or real smoke; a conclusion about probability-map planning; that the training domain per se, rather than the Level 2 cross-domain gap, causes the difference (Model A is evaluated in-domain on synthetic imagery, Model B cross-domain).

## 5. Level 2 to Level 3 linkage and seed-level view
`linkage.md` places each checkpoint's Level 2 quality on the synthetic positive test set (conf 0.25) beside its Level 3 behaviour (at its own Level 3 threshold). Model A: mAP50 0.995, recall 1.000, FP/image about 0.000 on all three seeds; Model B v2: mAP50 0.522-0.726, recall 0.405-0.589, FP/image 0.039-0.091. The ordering of families agrees at both levels: A is better on Level 2 mAP50, recall and precision, and on Level 3 first detection, false detections and coverage (B has more true detection observations per run, 73.9 vs 60.0, because it investigates and re-detects more cells). Within B, the seed with the best Level 2 mAP50 (seed 2) also has the earliest first true detection and the fewest false observations at Level 3, and seed 1 is worst on both; with 3 seeds this is an observation, not evidence of a relationship. The seed SDs (n = 3) in section 4 are small for A and larger for B; B2s1 includes the only run with no true detection (scenario 2004: 305 false detections observations, 0 true).

## 6. Right-censoring
Times are right-censored at 600 s. **Ignition-cell visit: 15 of 56 runs have no observed visit by 600 s** (baseline 0/8, A0 1/8, A1 0/8, A2 0/8, B2s0 4/8, B2s1 5/8, B2s2 5/8; by scenario 2006: 4, 2014: 3, 2015: 3, 2004: 2, 2012: 2, 2002: 1, 2001 and 2005: 0). **First true detection: 1 of 48 perception runs** (B2s1, scenario 2004) is censored. A censored run means the event was not seen by 600 s and the true time is greater than 600 s; the tables substitute 600 s only to form lower-bound means and the text labels them so. The observed-only median (baseline 207 s, A 236-260 s, B 247-427 s) is conditional on a visit and biased low for B; it is not the arm's typical time. No survival model was fitted. `censoring.md` has the full per-arm table, coverage means and per-scenario counts.

## 7. False-detection counts
`det_false` counts **repeated detection observations**: every ingested detection that fog scores incorrect, so one persistent false cell seen in many frames is counted many times, and the count scales with how long the UAV stays near it and with the 0.25 Hz perception rate. It is not a count of distinct false-alarm incidents. `per_run_extra.csv` adds the number of distinct cells with at least one false detection (B: 6.4 +- 1.2 per run, A: 0.2 +- 0.2), which is a closer but still imperfect proxy (adjacent cells counted separately, no time clustering). A true incident definition (for example contiguous-in-time and adjacent-cell grouping) was not applied. Likewise the INVESTIGATE columns count agent log lines (about one per second, not exactly one Hz and wall-clock, not simulated time), and "true" means near a cell that burned at any time during the run (time-agnostic), so false-target counts are lower bounds.

## 8. Repeatability (B2), confounded
One repeat each on scenario 2001: baseline ignition-cell first visit 87.0 s (sequential) vs 100.1 s (parallel), coverage 0.22 vs 0.18; A0 first true detection 101.2 vs 114.9 s, ignition-cell visit 108.3 vs 122.0 s, coverage 0.21 vs 0.19, true-target lines 59 vs 43. Both repeats are later by 13-14 s in the same direction, and **both repeats ran in parallel mode (and the A0 repeat on an unrecoverable intermediate harness) while both originals ran sequentially**. The difference therefore mixes run-to-run jitter, execution mode and harness state; it cannot be quoted as jitter. n = 1 per configuration. The earlier "about 20 s jitter" (pilot, sequential) is the only jitter figure not confounded in this way.

## 9. Findings and limitations for the report
Findings (descriptive): see section 4 "What these results support". Limitations to state: Model A is in-domain on synthetic imagery, Model B cross-domain (the contrast restates the Level 2 gap); smoke is a stack of flat billboards (no volume, no real 3D occlusion), flat ground, one pinhole camera, one UAV; 8 scenarios on 4 ignition cells and 3 seeds per family; no inference at the significance level the 4 clusters can support; reactive investigate-first policy with no probability map, no confidence-aware trade-off; Model B thresholds at the detector floor for 2 of 3 seeds (the planner receives near-all low-confidence boxes); one-cell truth radius; time-agnostic diversion truth; right-censoring at 600 s (15/56 ignition-cell visits); mixed sequential and parallel execution and an unrecoverable intermediate harness state for one repeat; SITL timing is not bit-reproducible (the scene, fire and detector are deterministic); detection counts are observations, not incidents; per-arm thresholds differ from the Level 2 operating point; a trajectory figure is illustrative only.
