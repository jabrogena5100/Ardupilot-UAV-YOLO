# Level 3 final paired evaluation (2026-11-09), corrected after the read-only audit

**Status: descriptive results prepared for independent Codex review. Not declared scientifically validated.** Revision note: the first version of this document (commit `7e203a1`) contained a provenance error (it said every run had `git_dirty: true`), reported unadjusted scenario-level p-values as if the 8 scenarios were independent, and treated censored times as means. Those are corrected here. A second audit then found a **fire-clock confound** (section 2a) and weak test coverage of the analysis code; the confound is documented, not fixed, and the tests were added. A third audit (Codex) found five further issues, corrected here: capped-time censoring was over-interpreted (sections 4 and 6), some fire-clock claims were not supported by the data (section 2a), the parallel-only subset covers 3 cells, not 4 (section 2a), the figure captions and axis labels overstated what was plotted (Artifacts), and one run had 9 detection records stamped after the 600 s window that were counted (section 7a). Passing tests show the code does what it is specified to do on known inputs; they do not establish that the experiment is scientifically valid. Earlier corrections: the audit that found them is summarised in `docs/handoffs/level3_final_audit.md`.

**Purpose.** Does the detector's training domain (Model A synthetic, Model B real/Boreal v2) change what a UAV does in ArduPilot SITL, against a no-detector patrol baseline? Protocol (frozen before any held-out run): `level3_methodology.md` section 15.3. Everything below is computed from recorded artifacts; generated tables come from `eval/level3_analyze.py` and `eval/level3_report_tables.py` (inputs and outputs listed under Artifacts).

**Reading rule (final audit, 2026-11-11).** Everything below describes observed behaviour under one implemented reactive, detection-triggered investigation policy, in 8 scenarios on 4 ignition cells. It does **not** establish an unbiased causal effect of the training domain (Model A is in-domain on synthetic imagery, Model B cross-domain; thresholds differ per arm; execution mode and realized fire trajectories differ, section 2a), a calibrated probabilistic-planning benefit (no probability map exists), or improved autonomous monitoring (neither perception family beat the plain patrol on average at reaching the ignition cell). Any intervals or p-values here are descriptive, not significance claims.

## 1. Design and what the "policy" is
8 held-out scenarios (2001, 2002, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2012, 2014, 2015) x 7 arms (baseline; A0, A1, A2; B2s0, B2s1, B2s2) = 56 runs, T = 600 s, single UAV, oracle off, 10 x 10 grid. Per-arm thresholds frozen (A 0.30-0.45, B 0.01-0.02).
**Only 4 distinct ignition cells exist** (`HELD_IGNITION_V2`, methodology 15.2): (3,2) in scenarios 2001 and 2005, (2,1) in 2002 and 2012, (1,0) in 2004 and 2014, (0,1) in 2006 and 2015. Within a cell the scenarios differ in fire seed and texture slot but share the ignition location, so for time-to-reach metrics they are partly pseudo-replicates. **The ignition cell is therefore the primary cluster (n = 4); scenarios (n = 8) are descriptive.**

**What flies the UAV is a reactive, detection-triggered investigation policy, not a probability-map planner.** Implemented and used (`planning/priority.py`, `swarm_agent.py` step 5): each detection above `conf_threshold` is stored as per-cell evidence with a 20 s lifetime; a cell's score is `max(conf)` plus a small repeat bonus; when any live evidence exists the UAV flies to the highest-scoring cell (ties by distance) **before** continuing the coverage patrol; on arrival the cell is cleared and re-flagging of that cell is suppressed for 60 s. Detections are scored against fog's ground truth at ingest (one-cell truth radius). Evidence of the mechanism: in the inspected runs (`l3-A0-2001`, `l3-B2s0-2004`) every INVESTIGATE target is a detected cell, the baseline has no detections and no investigations, and `oracle_hazards` is false in all 58 run manifests.
**Not implemented** (originally proposed in `CLAUDE.md`, Level 3 data flow): a geographic probability map (`mapping/probability_map.py` is an empty stub), detection probabilities combined over time or across looks (no log-odds update, no negative evidence, no use of Level 2 sensitivity/specificity), a priority-region decision from such a map, and any confidence-aware trade-off between investigating and covering (a detection at the 0.01 threshold floor diverts the UAV exactly like a confident one, the investigation always preempts coverage). Consequently the results measure how a fixed reactive policy turns each detector's output (including its false alarms) into behaviour; they do not measure the benefit of a probabilistic monitoring planner.

## 2a. Fire-clock confound (found in the second audit; documented, not fixed)
**Fixed configuration is not the same as an identical realized fire.** Every arm of a scenario has the same *initial fire configuration* (ignition cell, fire seed, `p_ignite` 0.0025, `burn_steps` 300, von Neumann, no wind) and this was verified from the configs. The *realized fire trajectory* differs: `fog_tracker.py` (lines 311-314) schedules each fire step one `dt` after the previous step *ran*, so the fire advances in wall-clock time plus loop latency, not in run-clock seconds, and takes fewer steps per second when fog's tick loop is delayed. Earlier wording in this project that the fire is "identical" or "deterministic" across arms is corrected to refer to the configuration only (`level3_methodology.md` sections 9 and 16).
**Measured** (`fire_clock.md`, generated from `fire_cells_*.csv` and `coverage_t_*.csv`; ratio = median ignition time of the run over the same-scenario baseline's, over cells burned in both):

| execution | arm group | runs | fire ratio mean (min-max) | fog ticks/s mean (min) | worst tick gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sequential | baseline | 4 | reference | 1.99 (1.99) | 0.5 s |
| sequential | A | 12 | 1.003 (1.001-1.006) | 1.98 (1.97) | 0.7 s |
| sequential | B | 10 | 1.004 (1.001-1.007) | 1.98 (1.97) | 0.6 s |
| 2-slot parallel | baseline | 4 | reference | 1.91 (1.90) | 1.4 s |
| 2-slot parallel | A | 12 | 1.058 (1.005-1.086) | 1.79 (1.73) | 21.4 s |
| 2-slot parallel | B | 14 | 1.050 (0.999-1.085) | 1.81 (1.75) | 22.3 s |

The nominal tick rate is 2/s. In the two parallel-mode scenarios where the final burned set differs between arms, scenario 2014 has 21 burned cells for the baseline and 15-16 for the perception arms, and scenario 2005 has 23 for B2s1 against 25 for the others; the other six scenarios have identical burned sets across all 7 arms.
**What the data support (measured in `fire_clock.md`).** (1) In the parallel scenarios the perception runs' ignitions are later than the same-scenario baseline's in 25 of 26 runs (mean ratio 1.058 for A, 1.050 for B; in the sequential scenarios the ratio is 1.001-1.007), and the final burned sets differ between arms in scenarios 2014 and 2005, so contrasts there compare unequal realized fires. (2) Fog's tick rate and the fire ratio are strongly rank-correlated (Spearman -0.84 for sequential and -0.87 for parallel perception runs), which is consistent with the wall-clock scheduling mechanism; this is an association, not proof of the mechanism. (3) The perception runs' lower tick rate than the baseline's in parallel mode (1.79-1.81 vs 1.91 per second) is consistent with, but does not show, a heavier load as the cause.
**What is not established.** The size or direction of any bias the fire clock introduces in the arm contrasts. The within-scenario association between a run's fire ratio and its first true detection time has inconsistent sign (+0.09, +0.37, -0.66, +0.03 in the four parallel scenarios with 6 runs each), so no effect on detection timing is demonstrated or excluded. The family difference in the fire ratio (A minus B) is small and mixed in sign over the four scenarios where both families ran in parallel mode (+0.007, +0.018, +0.010, -0.007): these data show no family-specific shift but cannot exclude one, and the earlier claim that the effect is "noise rather than bias" is withdrawn. The ratio is relative to the baseline run, whose own clock is also affected, so it is not an absolute clock error, and the earlier statements that the baseline's "fire ran fastest" and that fire-truth metrics "inherit" a difference were attributions that these data do not test. The ignition cell burns from t = 0 in every run, so its first-visit time does not depend on the fire clock directly (the route can). The offline fire calibration (methodology section 7) assumed an ideal one-step-per-second clock.
**Sensitivity (no new runs; `cluster_stats.md`).** Direction counts by execution era. The sequential-only scenarios (2001, 2002, 2004) cover **3 cells** ((3,2), (2,1), (1,0)); the parallel-only scenarios (2006, 2012, 2014, 2015) cover **3 cells** ((0,1), (2,1), (1,0)), because cell (3,2) is flown only in 2001 (sequential) and 2005 (mixed mode); cells (2,1) and (1,0) appear in both subsets and scenario 2005 is in neither. A-B first true detection, ignition-cell visit, coverage, false detection observations and distinct false cells point the same way in both subsets. **A versus baseline on the ignition-cell visit does not**: A is later in 3 of 3 sequential scenarios and cells, but in only 2 of 4 parallel scenarios (2 of 3 cells), so that contrast should not be called consistent (the overall 4/4-cell count mixes the two eras). B versus baseline (later in every subset) and the coverage deficits against the baseline agree in both eras. With 3 clusters per subset these counts are weak evidence on their own.
**Not done.** The protocol and raw runs are frozen and no new campaign was authorised. A small sequential rerun of the parallel scenarios (for example all 7 arms of 2014) would bound the effect; any future campaign should step the fire from the run clock or run sequentially.

## 3. Statistical approach and what it can support
The 4 ignition cells are the primary clusters. For each contrast the per-scenario differences (family value = mean over seeds) are averaged within a cell, giving 4 cell differences; the descriptive interval is a bootstrap over the 4 cells, and `p` is the exact sign-flip test over the 4 cell differences. **With 4 clusters the smallest possible two-sided p is 0.125, so no contrast can be significant at 0.05**, with or without Holm correction (16 contrasts; all Holm p >= 0.125). The p-values are reported only to make this explicit. Scenario-level numbers (8 differences, sign-flip floor 0.0078) are descriptive because scenarios within a cell are not independent. The earlier "p = 0.0078" statements are withdrawn. The supported evidence is direction and consistency: how many cells and scenarios have the same sign, and the size of the difference. Seeds are not pooled: a family value in a scenario is the mean of its 3 seeds, and seed spread is reported separately (section 5). The set of reported contrasts is the full set in `cluster_stats.md`; none was chosen after the fact, but with this sample size every interval is crude and uncertain.

## 4. Results (descriptive)

| first visit of ignition cell (s, capped mean; **15/56 censored**) | 221.4 | 268.0 +- 31.1 | 487.7 +- 14.4 |
| grid coverage at 600 s | 0.209 | 0.183 +- 0.003 | 0.147 +- 0.007 |
| false detection **observations** / run | 0 | 0.3 +- 0.2 | 55.7 +- 23.0 |

| INVESTIGATE log lines on burned-adjacent targets (about seconds) | 0 | 173.7 +- 10.1 | 237.1 +- 41.2 |
| INVESTIGATE log lines on false targets (about seconds) | 0 | 1.6 +- 2.7 | 80.6 +- 47.8 |

Direction (cell-level counts, then scenario-level), A relative to B: first true detection earlier in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (mean cell difference -119.5 s on capped values; descriptive cell-bootstrap interval -170.9 to -68.0; the only censored run is a B run, so the true gap is at least this large in magnitude); ignition-cell first visit earlier 4/4 and 8/8 (-219.7 s on capped values; B has 14 censored runs and A has 1, so censoring is not confined to one arm and this capped difference is **not** a bound on the true gap, which is probably larger in magnitude but is not determined by these data); coverage higher 4/4 and 8/8 (+0.035); false detection observations lower 4/4 and 8/8 (-55.4); distinct false cells lower 4/4 and 8/8 (-6.2). Time on true targets did not differ consistently (cells 2/2, scenarios 3/5; -63.4 log lines, interval -176.3 to +26.4). Against the baseline: B reached the ignition cell later in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (+266.3 s on capped values; the baseline is never censored and B is, so the true difference is at least this large); A reached it later in 4/4 cells but only 6/8 scenarios (+46.6 s on capped values, a lower bound on the true difference because only A is censored (1 run); in scenarios 2012 and 2015 A was earlier, by 32 s and 54 s), and the direction is era-dependent: 3/3 sequential scenarios, 2/4 parallel scenarios (section 2a); both perception arms covered less than the patrol in 4/4 cells and 8/8 scenarios (A -0.026, B -0.061). Scenario-level differences and the by-era sensitivity are in `cluster_stats.md`.

**What these results support:** Model A and Model B produce different behaviour under the same reactive policy, consistently in direction across all 4 cells: Model B (cross-domain, thresholds at the detector floor) detects later, raises many more false detection observations and false-target investigations, reaches the ignition cell later or not at all within 600 s, and covers less. On average neither perception arm found the ignition cell sooner than the plain patrol (A was earlier in 2 of 8 scenarios, both parallel-mode), so **no monitoring benefit of perception is shown here**; this is a property of the policy (investigations preempt coverage) as much as of the detectors.
**What they do not support:** significance claims; a quantity for the Model B delay (capped values are lower bounds on individual run times, not estimates); a statement about real flights or real smoke; a conclusion about probability-map planning; that the training domain per se, rather than the Level 2 cross-domain gap, causes the difference (Model A is evaluated in-domain on synthetic imagery, Model B cross-domain).

## 5. Level 2 to Level 3 linkage and seed-level view
`linkage.md` places each checkpoint's Level 2 quality on the synthetic positive test set (conf 0.25) beside its Level 3 behaviour (at its own Level 3 threshold). Model A: mAP50 0.995, recall 1.000, FP/image about 0.000 on all three seeds; Model B v2: mAP50 0.522-0.726, recall 0.405-0.589, FP/image 0.039-0.091. The ordering of families agrees at both levels: A is better on Level 2 mAP50, recall and precision, and on Level 3 first detection, false detections and coverage (B has more true detection observations per run, 73.5 vs 60.0, because it investigates and re-detects more cells). Within B, the seed with the best Level 2 mAP50 (seed 2) also has the earliest first true detection and the fewest false observations at Level 3, and seed 1 is worst on both; with 3 seeds this is an observation, not evidence of a relationship. The seed SDs (n = 3) in section 4 are small for A and larger for B; B2s1 includes the only run with no true detection (scenario 2004: 305 false detections observations, 0 true).

## 6. Right-censoring and capped values
Times are right-censored at the 600 s window. **Ignition-cell visit: 15 of 56 runs have no observed visit by 600 s** (baseline 0/8, A0 1/8, A1 0/8, A2 0/8, B2s0 4/8, B2s1 5/8, B2s2 5/8; by scenario 2006: 4, 2014: 3, 2015: 3, 2004: 2, 2012: 2, 2002: 1, 2001 and 2005: 0). **First true detection: 1 of 48 perception runs** (B2s1, scenario 2004) has no observed true detection. A censored run means the event was not seen in the window and the true time exceeds 600 s.
**How capped values may be read.** The tables and statistics substitute 600 s for an unobserved event (a *capped* value). Each capped value is a lower bound on that run's true time, so a capped arm mean is a lower bound on that arm's true mean. A capped *difference* between two arms is **not** in general a bound on the true difference. It is a one-sided bound only when the censoring is confined to the later arm: B-vs-baseline ignition-cell visit (the baseline is never censored), A-vs-baseline (only A has a censored run, 1) and the A-vs-B first true detection (the only censored run is a B run) satisfy this, so the true differences are at least as large in magnitude as the capped ones. The A-vs-B ignition-cell contrast does not (A has 1 censored run, B has 14), so its capped value of about -220 s is only descriptive. The bootstrap intervals and sign-flip p-values treat 600 s as an observed time and are not censoring-aware. The observed-only median (baseline 207 s, A 236-260 s, B 247-427 s) is conditional on a visit and biased low for B; it is not the arm's typical time. No survival model was fitted. `censoring.md` has the full per-arm table, coverage means and per-scenario counts.

## 7. False-detection counts
`det_false` counts **repeated detection observations**: every ingested detection that fog scores incorrect, so one persistent false cell seen in many frames is counted many times, and the count scales with how long the UAV stays near it and with the 0.25 Hz perception rate. It is not a count of distinct false-alarm incidents. `per_run_extra.csv` adds the number of distinct cells with at least one false detection (B: 6.4 +- 1.2 per run, A: 0.2 +- 0.2), which is a closer but still imperfect proxy (adjacent cells counted separately, no time clustering). A true incident definition (for example contiguous-in-time and adjacent-cell grouping) was not applied. Likewise the INVESTIGATE columns count agent log lines (about one per second, not exactly one Hz and wall-clock, not simulated time), and "true" means near a cell that burned at any time during the run (time-agnostic), so false-target counts are lower bounds.

## 7a. Measurement definitions and their limits
- **The 600 s window.** fog stops 0.01-0.94 s after 600 s (run end times in `experiment_end_*.txt`) and may log detections in that tail. Only detections stamped 0 <= t <= 600.0 s are counted. The first version of the analysis had no upper bound and counted 9 true-detection records stamped 600.01-600.16 s in `l3-B2s0-2014` (`det_true` was 76, now 67; `det_after_window` records them); no other run had late records, no first true detection or ignition-cell visit lies after 600 s, and `stats.json` is byte-identical. Coverage, the INVESTIGATE log lines and the burned counts are read at the run end (up to 0.94 s after the window), which is not separable from the logs.
- **Radius-one ground-truth matching.** A detection at cell (i, j) is scored *true* if **any cell within Chebyshev distance 1** (the 3 x 3 neighbourhood, `perception.truth_radius_cells: 1`, `fog/state.py::_is_correct`) is in the BURNING state at the moment of ingest; otherwise it is false. Consequences: a detection one cell away from the fire counts as true, so the first true detection can be credited from a neighbouring cell and localisation error is not assessed; detections of cells that have already burnt out (BURNT) are false; the scoring is on the cell grid (about 160 m per cell for the 1-mile, 10-cell grid), not on pixels. The true/false labels therefore describe cell-level agreement, and the false counts exclude near misses.
- **Observed-only censoring.** Means or medians computed over only the runs where the event was observed are conditional on the event having happened and are biased low, most for arms with many censored runs (ignition-cell visit: B 14 of 24 censored, A 1 of 24). The observed-only medians in `censoring.md` must not be compared across arms or read as typical times, and an observed-only A-B contrast drops entire cells (in the audit's scratch check, cell (0,1) had no observed B visits). The 600 s substitution is a lower bound, not an estimate, and no survival model was fitted.
- **INVESTIGATE log lines as a proxy for investigation time.** `inv_true_s` and `inv_false_s` count agent HUD log lines whose mode is INVESTIGATE. The HUD prints at most once per loop-time second and only when not in the final hold, the lines carry no timestamps, and the spacing varies with loop speed (the first harness-era runs print fewer than one line per second, for example `l3-A0-2004` has 581 lines in total), so the counts are approximately, not exactly, seconds and are not comparable between sequential and parallel runs without caution. An INVESTIGATE episode includes the approach flight and any dwell, so the counts are time spent diverted, not time spent looking. "True" is time-agnostic (target near a cell that burned at any point in the run) and, like the fire, depends on the realized fire trajectory (section 2a).

## 8. Repeatability (B2), confounded
One repeat each on scenario 2001: baseline ignition-cell first visit 87.0 s (sequential) vs 100.1 s (parallel), coverage 0.22 vs 0.18; A0 first true detection 101.2 vs 114.9 s, ignition-cell visit 108.3 vs 122.0 s, coverage 0.21 vs 0.19, true-target lines 59 vs 43. Both repeats are later by 13-14 s in the same direction, and **both repeats ran in parallel mode (and the A0 repeat on an unrecoverable intermediate harness) while both originals ran sequentially**. The difference therefore mixes run-to-run jitter, execution mode and harness state; it cannot be quoted as jitter. n = 1 per configuration. The earlier "about 20 s jitter" (pilot, sequential) is the only jitter figure not confounded in this way.

## 9. Findings and limitations for the report
Findings (descriptive): see section 4 "What these results support". Limitations to state: Model A is in-domain on synthetic imagery, Model B cross-domain (the contrast restates the Level 2 gap); smoke is a stack of flat billboards (no volume, no real 3D occlusion), flat ground, one pinhole camera, one UAV; 8 scenarios on 4 ignition cells and 3 seeds per family; no inference at the significance level the 4 clusters can support; reactive investigate-first policy with no probability map, no confidence-aware trade-off; Model B thresholds at the detector floor for 2 of 3 seeds (the planner receives near-all low-confidence boxes); radius-one ground-truth matching (section 7a); time-agnostic diversion truth; INVESTIGATE log lines are an approximate proxy for time (section 7a); observed-only censored summaries are biased low; right-censoring at 600 s (15/56 ignition-cell visits); mixed sequential and parallel execution, an unrecoverable intermediate harness state for one repeat, and a **fire-clock confound** (the initial fire configuration is identical across arms but the realized fire trajectory is not, strongest in parallel scenarios, section 2a); SITL timing is not bit-reproducible and fog schedules the fire by wall clock; detection counts are observations, not incidents; per-arm thresholds differ from the Level 2 operating point; a trajectory figure is illustrative only.
